This background is a live voice. Each glowing line is one moment of a sung note, split into its frequencies. Move your cursor across the screen, or use the sliders: sideways changes the pitch, upwards adds distortion, the same smearing my VoxScope research teaches machines to recognise.

I teach computers to hear what is inside a signal: the technique behind a singer's scream, the gesture hidden in a muscle twitch, the message buried in a noisy channel. I build the hardware too, from transformers wound by hand to robot arms.

Papers

One journal article and one conference paper, both about learning from signals.

IEEE Sensors Journal, 2026Early Access

Stacking Multi-Variate Time-Frequency Information: A Deep Learning Approach for IMU-MMG based Hand Gesture Recognition

Co-author. A deep learning model reads stacked time–frequency maps of motion and muscle signals to recognise hand gestures, so wearables and IoT devices can be controlled without a camera.

Figure: six sensor channels around one gesture, motion (IMU) above and muscle sound (MMG) below.

IEEE SPICSCON 2026Presented

VoxScope: A Deployment-Oriented Analysis Framework for Extreme Vocal Distortion Techniques in Heavy Metal

Handcrafted acoustic features and machine learning classify extreme vocal techniques, tested on singers the model has never heard, with recording-level predictions and a desktop app for real use.

Figure: spectrogram of a sung phrase that turns into a distorted scream halfway through.
